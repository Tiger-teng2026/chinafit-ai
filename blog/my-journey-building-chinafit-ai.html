From Zero to AI Tool: My Journey Building ChinaFit AI (My Second Website)

Last updated: August 28, 2026

I am, by any honest measure, completely illiterate when it comes to programming. I can't read a line of Python, and JavaScript makes my eyes glaze over. But I've always had this stubborn, burning desire to create things—apps, tools, websites—and I refused to let a little thing like not knowing how to code stop me.

The First Website: A Beautiful Disaster

It started with a simple idea and an unhealthy obsession. I spent an entire day and night talking to AI, back and forth, like a man mumbling to himself in a dark room. I was basically a self-styled lunatic, asking questions, pasting errors, trying again. And then, at 4 a.m., something magical happened: my first website went live.

I stared at that screen the way a kid stares at the final boss exploding in Contra—pure, unfiltered euphoria. I nodded at the webpage like I'd just painted the Sistine Chapel. I was convinced this was it. Overnight riches. Internet fame. The whole dream.

Spoiler alert: it wasn't. Nobody came. My brilliant website sank into the void, and I was left standing in the rain, soaked and confused.

The Lesson Behind the Failure

But here's the weird part: even in that failure, I felt a tiny flicker of excitement. It was the storm before the calm, I told myself. And there's a Chinese saying I kept repeating: "守得云开见月明" — roughly, "After the clouds part, you'll see the moonlight." I wasn't ready to give up.

Why I Built ChinaFit AI (My Second Act)

Around that time, I was also spending way too much money on clothes and shoes from Taobao and 1688. Every order was a gamble. The size charts were in Chinese, measured in centimeters, and I had no idea if a "42" would fit my foot or strangle it. I'd order, wait three weeks, and then end up with a shirt that fit like a crop top. The return shipping cost more than the item itself.

That's when it clicked. My first website failed because it didn't solve a real problem. This time, I would build something useful: an AI-powered tool that converts Chinese clothing and shoe sizes into US/EU/UK sizes, and even writes a message in Chinese to ask the seller for exact measurements.

The Build: Same Lunatic, Better Focus

So I went back to the AI. Day and night, same muttering, same trial and error. But this time I had clarity. I knew exactly what pain I was solving, because it was my own. I built the calculator, integrated DeepSeek AI for detailed breakdowns, added a paywall (because servers and AI calls aren't free), and launched it.

Was it easy? No. Did I want to throw my computer out the window at 3 a.m.? More than once. But every time I saw a user calculate their size and copy the Chinese message to send to a seller, I felt that same Contra boss-kill rush. Except this time, it was real. Real people. Real problems. Real value.

What I Learned (So You Don't Have To)

If you're an aspiring indie hacker who can't code, here's my advice: don't build a website just to build a website. Build something that scratches your own itch. Use AI as your co-pilot, but stay in the driver's seat. And when the first thing fails, laugh, get rained on, and start again. The moonlight will come.
