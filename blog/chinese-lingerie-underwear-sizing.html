Lingerie and Underwear Sizing – The Most Confusing Chinese Category

Last updated: September 3, 2026 · ~8 min read

Buying underwear and lingerie from China is the category Western shoppers get wrong most often — because the listing looks like it already did the conversion for you. A Taobao or AliExpress bralette chart says “M – fits US 34B.” You wear a 34B. You order the M. The band is so tight you cannot breathe, and the cups do not cover what a US 34B covers.

Chinese bra size conversion is a real system (70/75/80 + cup letters). The US size printed next to S/M/L on a lingerie listing is often not that system.

Chinese bra sizes: 70, 75, 80 are underbust in cm

Chinese bras (文胸) use underbust circumference in centimeters, rounded to 70, 75, 80, 85, 90. Cup letters (A, B, C, D, E…) come from the difference between bust and underbust. On a standard chart, that maps roughly to US even-number bands:

Chinese band Underbust (typical) US / UK band
70~68–72 cm32
75~73–77 cm34
80~78–82 cm36
85~83–87 cm38
90~88–92 cm40

So a Chinese 75B is on paper about a US 34B. A Chinese 70B is about a US 32B. That is the conversion. It is not the fit. Cups above D also diverge (Chinese E is often closer to US DD). Cheap bralettes ignore the system entirely and slap “M = 34B” on a stretchy triangle that was graded like a crop top.

Why a “correct” 75B still feels tighter

Asian average torsos are shorter, with a shallower ribcage and a narrower underbust even at a similar bust volume. A Chinese bra that matches your cup letter often has a snugger band than a US 34, and the cups sit differently because the wire and gore are built for that ribcage. Stretch in the band (most Taobao lingerie has some) makes it go on — then you get the same problem as stretchy clothing: it technically fits and is not comfortable.

If the chart says band 70cm and your underbust is 70cm, that is your starting size in the Chinese system — regardless of the US letter on the graphic. Then read reviews from Western buyers, because factory stretch-lace sets run smaller than the GB/T chart implies.

Underwear, boxers, and briefs run even smaller

Panties, boxers, and briefs do not use 70/75/80. They use S/M/L like clothing — and those letters run smaller than Chinese clothing letters, which are already smaller than US letters.

3XLOften the first size that fits a US L comfortably

Example: US M boxer briefs, Chinese XL on the chart. They arrived like a US S — tight waist, short leg. 3XL was the size that actually felt right. Same story as Chinese L vs US L, with less room to hide because underwear has almost no wearing ease.

How to order lingerie and underwear from China

  1. Forget S/M/L. Use band, bust, waist, and hip in centimeters. Compare them to your body, not to the US size on the infographic.
  2. For bras: measure underbust and bust in cm. Match those numbers to the seller’s 70/75/80 chart. Ignore “M = US 34B” unless the listing also shows those centimeters.
  3. For underwear: size up at least one full letter from what the chart suggests — often two if you wear a US M or L. If the chart says L, order XL; if it says XL, order 2XL. Then still check waist/hip cm.
  4. Read Western reviews. “I’m a US M and this 2XL fits perfectly” is a better converter than any flag graphic.
  5. Do not rely on stretch. Most Chinese lingerie has spandex. That is how it goes on, not how it should fit all day.

Hip and waist on Chinese charts are often flat (laid on the table). Double a flat hip, then add a little ease — even for stretch knits.

FAQ

Is Chinese 75B the same as US 34B?

As a conversion, yes: CN 75 ≈ US 34. As a wearable bra from a random Taobao shop, often no — expect a tighter band and a smaller cup volume. Match underbust cm first.

What if the listing only has S/M/L and no 70/75/80?

Treat it like a stretchy crop top, not a bra. Compare bust and underbust cm if they exist; otherwise size up and read reviews. No cm chart is a skip for anything with a band that has to close.

How do I convert Chinese underwear to US sizes?

Plan on 1–2 letters up from Chinese clothing conversion. A US L is frequently a Chinese 2XL or 3XL in underwear. Waist and hip in cm beat every letter.
