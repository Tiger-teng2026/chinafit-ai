Chinese Shoe Sizes vs. EU Sizes – They Look the Same, But They're Not

Last updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

You see a pair of sneakers from China. Size 44. You wear an EU 44 in European brands. You order a 44.

Big mistake.

Chinese shoe sizes and EU sizes are not the same – even though the numbers look identical. A Chinese 44 fits a 270mm foot. A real EU 44 fits a 280mm foot. That's a full centimeter difference – enough to turn a comfortable shoe into a toe-crushing nightmare.

Chinese 44 is not EU 44

I learned this lesson the expensive way. Found a pair of boots I loved. Saw “size 43” and thought, “Great, I'm an EU 43.” When they arrived, I couldn't even get my foot past the opening. I measured the insole – it was a full size smaller than any EU 43 I'd ever worn.

Why the conversion isn't exact

Why does this happen? Because China has its own shoe sizing standard. They measure foot length in centimeters and multiply by 10 – so a 250mm foot is a Chinese size 250. But on international listings, they often convert that to a number that looks like EU sizing. The conversion isn't exact. It's close enough to fool you, but not close enough to fit you.

The width problem: Asian lasts

And then there's the width problem. Chinese shoes are built on Asian lasts – narrower, with a higher arch. Even if the length is right, a Western foot with average width will feel squeezed. A Chinese 42 might be the right length for you, but it'll feel like you're wearing a vise.

I've talked to so many people who swear off Chinese shoes entirely because they ordered once, got the size wrong, and never tried again. But it's not that complicated once you know the trick.

The only reliable way to buy Chinese shoes

Measure your foot in centimeters. Stand on a piece of paper, mark your heel and your longest toe, measure the distance. That's your foot length. Then match that number to the seller's cm column, not the EU number.

  • If the chart says “size 42 = 26cm” and your foot is 26cm, order the 42.
  • If the chart only gives EU numbers without cm equivalents – ask the seller for the actual insole length in centimeters. If they won't give it, don't buy.
  • If you have wide feet? Go up half a size or even a full size. Trust me on this one.

The numbers on the tag are just suggestions. The centimeters on the chart are the truth. Learn to read them, and you'll stop wasting money on shoes that don't fit.

FAQ

Can I order my usual EU size from a Chinese seller?

Don't. A Chinese 44 and an EU 44 look identical on the listing and are often a full centimeter apart. Match foot length in cm (or insole mm) instead.

What should I ask the seller?

Ask for 鞋垫长度 (insole length) in centimeters or millimeters. Compare that to your foot length plus 5–10mm of toe room.
