Flat Measurements vs. Body Measurements – Why That Size Chart Is Lying to You

Last updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

You find a shirt on AliExpress. The size chart says chest: 52cm. You measure your chest – 104cm. Perfect, right? You order a Large. It arrives and it's so tight you can barely breathe.

What happened?

You just fell for the oldest trap in Chinese sizing: flat measurements vs. body measurements. And nobody warns you about it.

What “chest 52cm” actually means

Here's the deal. When a Chinese seller lists “chest 52cm” on their size chart, they're not telling you that the shirt fits a 52cm chest. They're telling you that if you lay the shirt flat on a table and measure from armpit to armpit, it's 52cm. Double that, and you get 104cm – which is the garment's circumference, not your body's circumference.

Sounds like the same thing, right? It's not. Not even close.

Why garment circumference is not your body size

A garment needs extra room – what the industry calls ease – for you to actually move in it. Breathe. Raise your arms. Sit down without popping a button. If the garment measures 104cm around and your body measures 104cm around, you're wearing a painted-on shirt. You need that garment to be 10–15cm larger than your body just to feel normal.

I learned this the hard way

I bought a jacket from China, checked the chart religiously, matched every number to my body measurements. When it showed up, I couldn't zip it past my sternum. I thought I'd gained weight overnight. Nope – I just didn't know I was comparing apples to oranges.

How to read a Chinese size chart correctly

Look for the words “flat measurement” or “garment measurement” on the chart. If you see those, you're looking at the clothes' measurements, not your body's. That means you need to add a few centimeters of breathing room to your own numbers before you match them.

If the chart doesn't specify, assume it's a flat measurement – most Chinese sellers use this system because it's faster and easier for factories.

The foolproof method: measure a shirt you already own

And if you really want to play it safe? Find a shirt you already own that fits you perfectly. Lay it flat. Measure armpit to armpit. Double it. Now you know exactly what garment measurement works for your body. Use that number when you shop Chinese sizes, and suddenly the whole system starts making sense.

  1. Grab a shirt that already fits.
  2. Lay it flat. Measure armpit to armpit.
  3. Double that number — that's your target garment chest.
  4. Match that number on the seller's chart, not S/M/L, and not your body tape.

Stop trusting the letters. Stop trusting your body measurements alone. Learn the flat-measurement game, and you'll stop wasting money on clothes that don't fit.

FAQ

Is chest 52cm my body size or the shirt's size?

On most Chinese charts, it is the shirt laid flat: armpit to armpit. Double it (104cm) to get garment circumference, then add 10–15cm of ease on top of your body chest.

What if the number is already ~100cm?

If adult chest/bust is in the 90–120cm range, it may already be circumference. If it is 46–62cm, it is almost certainly flat. When in doubt, ask the seller: “平铺还是围度?” (flat or circumference?).
