The Height-Weight Size Chart Trap – Why “175cm / 70kg” Means Nothing

Last updated: August 29, 2026 · ~6 min read

You're scrolling through a Chinese store. You see a size chart that looks dead simple: height and weight. “Size L – fits 170–175cm, 65–75kg.” You're 172cm and 68kg. Perfect fit, right?

Wrong. So wrong.

I ordered a hoodie from a seller who claimed their XL fit 175–180cm, 65–80kg. I'm 172cm and 63kg – well within that range, maybe even a little smaller. When it arrived, I could barely get it over my shoulders. It felt like it was made for someone four sizes smaller.

Why height-weight charts fail Western bodies

Here's the thing about Chinese height-weight charts: they're based on Asian body proportions. An Asian person who is 175cm and 70kg has a very different build than a Westerner with the same height and weight. Narrower shoulders. Smaller chest. Shorter arms. The chart isn't wrong – it's just calibrated for a different population.

That's why a “fits 175cm / 70kg” Large can still crush a Western XL. Weight doesn't tell you shoulder width. Height doesn't tell you sleeve length. For more on that mismatch, see Chinese L vs US L.

The 斤 (jin) trap: not pounds, not kilograms

And that's not even the worst part. Some Chinese sellers use jin (斤) instead of kilograms. One jin equals 0.5 kg. So if you see “90–115斤” and think that's 90–115 pounds, you're in for a nasty surprise. That's actually 45–57.5 kg – about 100–127 pounds. If you're an average-sized Western adult, you're already outside their entire size range before you even start.

The red flag: charts with no chest, shoulder, or length

I've seen so many people fall for this. They see a chart that looks user-friendly – just height and weight, no complicated measurements – and they think it's foolproof. Then they spend weeks waiting for a package that's going to end up in the donation pile.

Here's my rule: never buy based on height and weight alone. Those charts are a rough guess at best, and for Western bodies, they're usually way off. If a seller only provides height and weight without chest, shoulder, and length measurements, that's a red flag. Ask for the actual centimeter measurements. If they won't provide them, move on to another seller.

And when they do send centimeters, remember those are often flat garment measurements, not your body.

If you absolutely must use height-weight: size up

And if you absolutely must use a height-weight chart? Size up at least one full size from what they recommend. Maybe two. That hoodie I bought? I should have ordered a 3XL or 4XL, not an XL. The chart said XL, but my body said “try again.”

Your height and weight don't tell the whole story. Your chest, shoulders, and waist do. Use those numbers, not the ones on the lazy chart.

FAQ

Does 175cm / 70kg mean I should order a Chinese L?

Not if you have a Western build. That recommendation is for a narrower Asian frame at the same stats. Ask for chest, shoulder, and length in cm, or size up 1–2 letters.

Is 斤 the same as a pound or a kilogram?

Neither. 1 斤 = 0.5 kg ≈ 1.10 lbs. Mixing 斤 with pounds roughly doubles the size you think you are buying.
