A Chinese “L” Is Not the Same as an American “L”

Last updated: August 27, 2026 · ~5 min read

Three hoodies from three Chinese sellers, all labeled XL: one fits normal, one is snug, and the third barely covers a 5'11" torso. Same letter, completely different reality. A Chinese “L” is not a US “L” — and it is not even close.

The golden rule: Chinese sizes run one to two full letters smaller than US sizes. If you wear a US M, order at least a Chinese L; an XL is safer. As one shopper put it: don’t trust the letter — Asian sizing is way smaller than European or American.

Why the gap exists

  • Chinese patterns are built for leaner, shorter frames with narrower shoulders.
  • US brands use vanity sizing (a bigger garment, a smaller letter). Chinese brands generally do not.
  • There is almost no factory-to-factory consistency. One seller’s L is another’s M.

Forget the letter. Every time, hunt down the cm size chart and compare chest, shoulder, and length. No chart? Skip it. Full letter chart: Chinese S/M/L/XL/2XL to US.
