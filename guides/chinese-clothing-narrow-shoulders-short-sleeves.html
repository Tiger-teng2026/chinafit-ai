Clothing Guide

The Shoulders Are Too Narrow, and the Sleeves Are Too Short

Last updated: August 27, 2026 · ~5 min read

A US M t-shirt sits right at the shoulders and sleeves. A Chinese XL jacket can still have a fine chest — and feel like a kid’s shirt across the back, with sleeves ending well above the wrist. That is not a “wrong size.” It is a different fit.

Chinese garments are cut for Asian proportions: narrower shoulders, shorter torsos, shorter arms. Sizing up helps chest more than it helps shoulder width and sleeve length. A Chinese XL might match a US M chest while the shoulders stay narrower than a US M.

What to check besides chest

MeasurementChinese (typical vs US)Why it matters
Shoulder (肩宽)Often 1–3 cm narrowerPinch and “kid’s shirt” feel
Sleeve (袖长)Typically 2–3 cm shorterCuffs ride up
Length (衣长)Shorter bodyHem sits high if you are tall

If you are over 5'10", expect sleeve issues. Over 6'1", skip regular Chinese sizing and look for oversized / loose-fit or “big and tall” sellers. No 肩宽 or 袖长 on the chart? Message the seller or move on.

Glossary: how to read 平铺胸围, 衣长, 肩宽. If the listing is “unisex,” the shoulder and sleeve problem is often a women’s or Asian men’s block in disguise — women’s vs men’s vs unisex charts.
