Clothing Guide

Every Chinese Seller Seems to Have Their Own “L”

Last updated: August 27, 2026 · ~4 min read

Three L t-shirts, three sellers, one week: one perfect, one pajama-baggy, one a nine-year-old could swim in. There is no unified, enforced letter standard. Europe has EN 13402, the US has ASTM. China’s GB/T 1335 exists — and is not mandatory. Factories use their own charts. One shop’s L is another’s XL.

Why the industry is this messy

Factory typeWhat “L” often means
Domestic marketSmaller Asian cut
Export / WesternSometimes adjusted — not always correctly
Trying to do bothLetters that match neither chart

Thousands of small factories, each doing their own thing. You never know which you are getting from the letter alone.

The only real rule

  • Do not trust the letter on the tag.
  • Every order: match the detailed cm chart to your body (and to a shirt you own).
  • No chart? Ask. Vague numbers or no reply? Don’t buy. That is protecting your wallet, not being picky.
